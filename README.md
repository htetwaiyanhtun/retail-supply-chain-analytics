### Project Background & Purpose

Rather than practicing on clean, pre-packaged tutorial datasets, I wanted to tackle real-world business puzzles using modern enterprise tools. Working with the **DataCo Smart Supply Chain dataset (~180,000 transaction records)**, I focused on core operational and commercial friction points: **phantom revenue, warehouse stock concentration, logistics bottlenecks, and customer churn.**

Throughout this project, I used an **AI tool as an analytical thought partner and sparring mentor**. We debated data modeling architecture, reviewed SQL logic for subtle join/aggregation traps, and stress-tested edge cases. Crucially, all SQL views, DAX measures, and data models were authored, executed, and validated directly on a hosted cloud database instance.

### 1. Catching $1.41M in "Ghost Revenue"

Before building reports or tracking high-level KPIs, auditing raw transaction integrity was essential:

* **The Top-Line Illusion:** Raw booked sales indicated **$33.05M** across ~66K orders.
* **The Reality:** Digging into order statuses revealed **2,855 non-viable transactions** marked as `CANCELED` (1,367) or `SUSPECTED_FRAUD` (1,488)—representing **$1.41M in unrealized revenue**.
* **Database Fix:** Rather than applying cosmetic filters in reporting tools, these non-viable records were eliminated upstream within modular PostgreSQL analytical views.
* **Clean Baseline:** Established a validated production baseline of **$31.64M net revenue** across **63K fulfilled orders**.

### 2. The Grouping Blunder & Granularity Breakthrough

Early in the customer analysis, I made an analytical misstep: grouping customers by geographic attributes (`market`, `order_country`) alongside customer details.

* **The Problem:** Orders from single customers who purchased across different regions were split into separate rows. This artificially fragmented repeat buyers, falsely suggesting that ~80% of our customers bought only once or twice.
* **The Correction:** Recognizing the granularity mismatch, I refactored the SQL grouping strictly around `customer_id`.
* **True Baseline:** The unified aggregation uncovered **20,652 unique customers** with an actual **56.98% repeat purchase rate** (with nearly 30% ordering between 5 and 15 times).

### 3. Customer RFM: Why Empirical Percentiles Beat Textbook NTILE

Standard tutorials often prescribe using `NTILE(5)` or arbitrary 30/60/90-day activity buckets. Testing those assumptions against real data quickly broke down:

* **The NTILE Trap:** Because `NTILE` forces data into rigid 20% bins, it categorized historically heavy spenders who hadn't purchased in over 9 months as top-tier "Champions."
* **The 30-Day Mismatch:** This business sells durable goods like kayaks, fitness equipment, and camping tents. Customers naturally do not replace outdoor equipment every 30 days.

**The Solution:**  
We set aside theoretical rules and calculated **empirical distribution percentiles** (P25, P50, P75, P90) directly from the transaction dataset:
* **Natural Repurchase Cadence:** Customer purchase intervals naturally cluster between **120 and 150 days (4–5 months)**, aligning closely with the median recency (P50 at ~159 days).
* **Statistical Deviation vs. Definitive Churn:**
  * **P25 (~75 days):** Benchmark for active, engaged buyers within their normal cycle.
  * **P75 (~307 days):** Crossing this threshold does **not** confirm permanent churn. When inactivity extends to roughly double the median cycle (~10 months), it marks an **elevated risk window**. The customer has clearly stalled their natural cadence, making this the prime intervention zone for automated re-engagement.
  * **P90+ (>500 days / ~1.3+ years):** This marks the operational boundary for **true dormant/lost accounts**, where engagement has ceased entirely.

**The $15.5M Churn Wake-Up Call:**  
This empirical view revealed that **$15.5M in cumulative historical revenue** (roughly 47% of total company sales history) was generated by customers who have drifted into disengagement:
* **High-Value At Risk ($11.44M historical spend):** Averaged 6.1 orders but inactive ~8.5 months (past median cadence, approaching P75)—representing the primary window for retention intervention.
* **Lost High-Value ($4.07M historical spend):** Averaged 5.6 orders but inactive >1.3 years (past P90)—representing deeply dormant accounts.

*(Note: This $15.5M does not guarantee future recoverable revenue; it quantifies the massive pool of historical customer value currently exposed to churn).*

### 4. Logistics Fulfillment & The "Average Trap"

Analyzing fulfillment operations across ~63K shipments revealed unexpected operational breakdowns:

* **The Premium Shipping Paradox:** Customers paying extra for **First Class shipping experienced a 0.00% On-Time Delivery rate** across ~28,000 shipments, missing scheduled delivery dates by an average of 1.0 full day.
* **The "Average Trap" in Standard Class:** Standard Class showed an average lead time variance of `0.00 days`. However, early deliveries (-1 to -2 days) were mathematically canceling out late deliveries (+1 to +2 days) in the mean. A binary compliance check revealed that **39.77% of Standard orders actually arrived late**. In operations, never rely on net averages alone without checking binary compliance.

### 5. Warehouse Concentration (Pareto ABC Analysis)

Using PostgreSQL window functions, catalog revenue contribution was mapped into ABC tiers:
* **Class A SKUs:** Just **7 products (5.9% of the catalog)** generate **76.87% ($24.3M) of total net revenue**. Strict zero-stockout safety stock policies are vital for this small cohort.
* **Class C SKUs:** 95 products (80.5% of the catalog) contribute barely ~5% of sales, **indicating potential opportunities for inventory optimization and further SKU-level review**.

### Technical Architecture & Stack

* **PostgreSQL (Aiven Cloud Instance):** Modular analytical views (`v_financial_performance`, `v_logistics_delivery_health`, `v_pareto_abc_analysis`, `v_customer_rfm_segmentation`) with explicit date type-casting (`::date`) for robust BI integration.
* **Query Workbench:** DBeaver for query design, execution, and data validation.
* **Power BI Desktop:** Built around an explicit Star Schema (Fact tables linked to a continuous DAX `CALENDAR()` Date Dimension and analytical dimensions) with a centralized DAX `_Measures` table and an executive single-page interface.
* **AI Collaboration:** Used as an interactive sparring partner for code review, edge-case testing, and logic verification, while all queries, data modeling, and validations were completed directly by me.
